Tonkinese: healthy weight, feeding chart and care

A Tonkinese is healthy at 6–12 lb (2.7–5.5 kg) as an adult and typically lives 14–18 years. At 9 lb, a spayed or neutered adult needs about 241 kcal a day, roughly 0.67 cups of a typical dry food, and 2–3 sessions of 20–30 minutes of play.

Healthy Tonkinese weight

The healthy adult range is 6–12 lb (2.7–5.5 kg). Individual frames differ, so treat the range as a guide and judge by body condition instead of the scale alone.

Ribs felt without pressing. Waist visible behind the ribs, abdomen tucked. This is the target. That is a body condition score of 5 on the 9-point scale vets use. See all nine scores and find where your cat sits.

A kitten of this breed is still growing until about 1 year. Until then, weigh weekly and follow the trend with your vet instead of aiming at an adult number.

What Tonkineses are prone to

A predisposition is not a diagnosis. Most Tonkineses never develop most of these. Knowing the list tells you what to screen for and what to raise with your vet.

  • AsthmaWatch for signs

    Coughing that looks like a hairball but produces nothing. Reduce dust, litter dust, and aerosols.

  • GingivitisWatch for signs

    Red, painful gum margins. Common in this breed young; needs proactive dental care.

  • AmyloidosisScreening available

    Protein deposits damaging kidneys or liver in certain breeds. Annual bloodwork.

  • Progressive retinal atrophyScreening available

    Inherited, gradual blindness. A DNA test exists for many breeds; night-vision loss is the first sign.

  • Dental diseaseWatch for signs

    Most pets have periodontal disease by age three. Daily brushing plus professional cleaning under anesthesia is what actually works.

  • How to use this listWatch for signs

    These are breed tendencies drawn from population data, not findings in your animal. Most dogs and cats of any breed never develop most of these. Bring the list to your next appointment and ask which two are worth screening for at this age.

Go to a vet now if you see this

  • Trouble breathing, open-mouth breathing in a cat, or gums that are blue, white or brick red
  • Repeated unproductive retching, a swollen or drum-tight belly, restlessness and pacing
  • Collapse, sudden weakness, or a seizure lasting over two minutes or repeating
  • Straining in the litter box or unable to pass urine — in male cats this is fatal within a day
  • Bleeding that will not stop, or a suspected broken bone
  • Suspected poisoning: chocolate, xylitol, grapes, lilies for cats, antifreeze, human medication

Common questions

How much should a Tonkinese weigh?

A healthy adult Tonkinese usually weighs 6–12 lb (2.7–5.5 kg). The number matters less than body condition: you should feel the ribs without pressing and see a waist from above.

How much should I feed a Tonkinese?

A spayed or neutered adult at 9 lb needs about 241 kcal a day, which is roughly 0.67 cups of a 360 kcal-per-cup food split into 3 meals. Check the calories per cup on your own bag, because foods range from under 300 to over 450.

How long do Tonkineses live?

Typically 14–18 years. They count as senior from about age 11, when twice-yearly vet visits start to pay off.

How much play does a Tonkinese need?

About 2–3 sessions of 20–30 minutes a day for a healthy adult. End each session with a catch and a meal — hunt, catch, eat, groom, sleep is the sequence cats are built around.

What health problems are Tonkineses prone to?

The ones to know about are asthma, gingivitis, amyloidosis, progressive retinal atrophy, dental disease and how to use this list. A predisposition is not a diagnosis, but it tells you what to screen for and what to mention to your vet.
