Pomeranian: healthy weight, feeding chart and care

A Pomeranian is healthy at 3–8 lb (1.5–3.5 kg) as an adult and typically lives 12–16 years. At 5.5 lb, a spayed or neutered adult needs about 222 kcal a day, roughly 0.62 cups of a typical dry food, and 45–60 minutes of exercise.

Healthy Pomeranian weight

The healthy adult range is 3–8 lb (1.5–3.5 kg). Individual frames differ, so treat the range as a guide and judge by body condition instead of the scale alone.

Ribs felt without pressing. Waist visible behind the ribs, abdomen tucked. This is the target. That is a body condition score of 5 on the 9-point scale vets use. See all nine scores and find where your dog sits.

A puppy of this breed is still growing until about 9 months. Until then, weigh weekly and follow the trend with your vet instead of aiming at an adult number.

What Pomeranians are prone to

A predisposition is not a diagnosis. Most Pomeranians never develop most of these. Knowing the list tells you what to screen for and what to raise with your vet.

  • Tracheal collapseWatch for signs

    A weak windpipe causing a honking cough. Harness only, keep weight down, avoid smoke.

  • Patellar luxationWatch for signs

    Kneecaps that slip out of the groove, causing a skipping gait. Grade it at the annual exam before it becomes arthritis.

  • Dental diseaseWatch for signs

    Most pets have periodontal disease by age three. Daily brushing plus professional cleaning under anesthesia is what actually works.

  • Alopecia XWatch for signs

    Coat loss over the trunk and tail in plush-coated breeds, sparing the head and legs. Rule out thyroid and adrenal disease first.

  • HypoglycemiaWatch for signs

    Toy puppies can crash their blood sugar. Small frequent meals until six months.

  • How to use this listWatch for signs

    These are breed tendencies drawn from population data, not findings in your animal. Most dogs and cats of any breed never develop most of these. Bring the list to your next appointment and ask which two are worth screening for at this age.

Go to a vet now if you see this

  • Trouble breathing, open-mouth breathing in a cat, or gums that are blue, white or brick red
  • Repeated unproductive retching, a swollen or drum-tight belly, restlessness and pacing — this is bloat until proven otherwise
  • Collapse, sudden weakness, or a seizure lasting over two minutes or repeating
  • Bleeding that will not stop, or a suspected broken bone
  • Suspected poisoning: chocolate, xylitol, grapes, lilies for cats, antifreeze, human medication
  • Sudden inability to use the hind legs, or crying out when touched along the back

Common questions

How much should a Pomeranian weigh?

A healthy adult Pomeranian usually weighs 3–8 lb (1.5–3.5 kg). The number matters less than body condition: you should feel the ribs without pressing and see a waist from above.

How much should I feed a Pomeranian?

A spayed or neutered adult at 5.5 lb needs about 222 kcal a day, which is roughly 0.62 cups of a 360 kcal-per-cup food split into 2 meals. Check the calories per cup on your own bag, because foods range from under 300 to over 450.

How long do Pomeranians live?

Typically 12–16 years. They count as senior from about age 11, when twice-yearly vet visits start to pay off.

How much exercise does a Pomeranian need?

About 45–60 minutes a day for a healthy adult. Sniffing walks tire a dog more than distance. Let them stop.

What health problems are Pomeranians prone to?

The ones to know about are tracheal collapse, patellar luxation, dental disease, alopecia x, hypoglycemia and how to use this list. A predisposition is not a diagnosis, but it tells you what to screen for and what to mention to your vet.
