Tibetan Terrier: healthy weight, feeding chart and care

A Tibetan Terrier is healthy at 18–31 lb (8–14 kg) as an adult and typically lives 12–15 years. At 25 lb, a spayed or neutered adult needs about 692 kcal a day, roughly 1.92 cups of a typical dry food, and 45–60 minutes of exercise.

Healthy Tibetan Terrier weight

The healthy adult range is 18–31 lb (8–14 kg). Individual frames differ, so treat the range as a guide and judge by body condition instead of the scale alone.

Ribs felt without pressing. Waist visible behind the ribs, abdomen tucked. This is the target. That is a body condition score of 5 on the 9-point scale vets use. See all nine scores and find where your dog sits.

A puppy of this breed is still growing until about 1 year. Until then, weigh weekly and follow the trend with your vet instead of aiming at an adult number.

What Tibetan Terriers are prone to

A predisposition is not a diagnosis. Most Tibetan Terriers never develop most of these. Knowing the list tells you what to screen for and what to raise with your vet.

  • Progressive retinal atrophyScreening available

    Inherited, gradual blindness. A DNA test exists for many breeds; night-vision loss is the first sign.

  • Hip dysplasiaScreening available

    Loose hip joints that grind into arthritis. Ask about OFA or PennHIP screening; keeping them lean is the single biggest lever you control.

  • Primary lens luxationScreening available

    The lens slips out of place and causes sudden, painful glaucoma. There is a DNA test; a red, cloudy, painful eye is a same-day emergency.

  • Patellar luxationWatch for signs

    Kneecaps that slip out of the groove, causing a skipping gait. Grade it at the annual exam before it becomes arthritis.

  • CataractsScreening available

    Cloudy lens, often reversible with surgery. Also screen for diabetes when they appear suddenly.

  • How to use this listWatch for signs

    These are breed tendencies drawn from population data, not findings in your animal. Most dogs and cats of any breed never develop most of these. Bring the list to your next appointment and ask which two are worth screening for at this age.

Go to a vet now if you see this

  • Trouble breathing, open-mouth breathing in a cat, or gums that are blue, white or brick red
  • Repeated unproductive retching, a swollen or drum-tight belly, restlessness and pacing — this is bloat until proven otherwise
  • Collapse, sudden weakness, or a seizure lasting over two minutes or repeating
  • Bleeding that will not stop, or a suspected broken bone
  • Suspected poisoning: chocolate, xylitol, grapes, lilies for cats, antifreeze, human medication
  • Sudden inability to use the hind legs, or crying out when touched along the back

Common questions

How much should a Tibetan Terrier weigh?

A healthy adult Tibetan Terrier usually weighs 18–31 lb (8–14 kg). The number matters less than body condition: you should feel the ribs without pressing and see a waist from above.

How much should I feed a Tibetan Terrier?

A spayed or neutered adult at 25 lb needs about 692 kcal a day, which is roughly 1.92 cups of a 360 kcal-per-cup food split into 2 meals. Check the calories per cup on your own bag, because foods range from under 300 to over 450.

How long do Tibetan Terriers live?

Typically 12–15 years. They count as senior from about age 9, when twice-yearly vet visits start to pay off.

How much exercise does a Tibetan Terrier need?

About 45–60 minutes a day for a healthy adult. Sniffing walks tire a dog more than distance. Let them stop.

What health problems are Tibetan Terriers prone to?

The ones to know about are progressive retinal atrophy, hip dysplasia, primary lens luxation, patellar luxation, cataracts and how to use this list. A predisposition is not a diagnosis, but it tells you what to screen for and what to mention to your vet.
