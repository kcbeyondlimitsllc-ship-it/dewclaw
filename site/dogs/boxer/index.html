Boxer: healthy weight, feeding chart and care

Healthy Boxer weight

The healthy adult range is 55–71 lb (25–32 kg). Individual frames differ, so treat the range as a guide and judge by body condition instead of the scale alone.

Ribs felt without pressing. Waist visible behind the ribs, abdomen tucked. This is the target. That is a body condition score of 5 on the 9-point scale vets use. See all nine scores and find where your dog sits.

A puppy of this breed is still growing until about 1.3 years. Until then, weigh weekly and follow the trend with your vet instead of aiming at an adult number.

What Boxers are prone to

A predisposition is not a diagnosis. Most Boxers never develop most of these. Knowing the list tells you what to screen for and what to raise with your vet.

  • Dilated cardiomyopathyScreening available

    The heart muscle thins and stretches. Ask about a screening echocardiogram and Holter monitor.

  • Mast cell tumorsWatch for signs

    Any new skin lump on this breed deserves a needle aspirate rather than a wait-and-see.

  • Subaortic stenosisScreening available

    A congenital narrowing below the aortic valve. Any murmur in a young dog of this breed deserves an echo.

  • Brachycephalic airway syndromeWatch for signs

    A short skull with normal-length soft tissue means every breath is work. Avoid heat and exertion; loud snoring and exercise intolerance are surgical red flags, not personality.

  • HypothyroidismScreening available

    Low thyroid causing weight gain, coat loss, and lethargy. A cheap blood test finds it; a daily pill fixes it.

  • How to use this listWatch for signs

    These are breed tendencies drawn from population data, not findings in your animal. Most dogs and cats of any breed never develop most of these. Bring the list to your next appointment and ask which two are worth screening for at this age.

Go to a vet now if you see this

  • Trouble breathing, open-mouth breathing in a cat, or gums that are blue, white or brick red
  • Repeated unproductive retching, a swollen or drum-tight belly, restlessness and pacing — this is bloat until proven otherwise
  • Collapse, sudden weakness, or a seizure lasting over two minutes or repeating
  • Bleeding that will not stop, or a suspected broken bone
  • Suspected poisoning: chocolate, xylitol, grapes, lilies for cats, antifreeze, human medication
  • Sudden inability to use the hind legs, or crying out when touched along the back

Common questions

How much should a Boxer weigh?

A healthy adult Boxer usually weighs 55–71 lb (25–32 kg). The number matters less than body condition: you should feel the ribs without pressing and see a waist from above.

How much should I feed a Boxer?

A spayed or neutered adult at 63 lb needs about 1,384 kcal a day, which is roughly 3.84 cups of a 360 kcal-per-cup food split into 2 meals. Check the calories per cup on your own bag, because foods range from under 300 to over 450.

How long do Boxers live?

Typically 10–12 years. They count as senior from about age 7.5, when twice-yearly vet visits start to pay off.

How much exercise does a Boxer need?

About 60–90 minutes a day for a healthy adult. Sniffing walks tire a dog more than distance. Let them stop.

What health problems are Boxers prone to?

The ones to know about are dilated cardiomyopathy, mast cell tumors, subaortic stenosis, brachycephalic airway syndrome, hypothyroidism and how to use this list. A predisposition is not a diagnosis, but it tells you what to screen for and what to mention to your vet.

Sources and method

These figures are computed, not written by hand, from Dewclaw's breed data. How we calculate has the formulas and every factor.

  • Energy needs: resting energy requirement, 70 × (kg)0.75, times a life-stage factor, as in the WSAVA Global Nutrition Guidelines.
  • Body condition: the WSAVA 9-point body condition score.
  • Life stages: AAHA canine and AAHA/AAFP feline life stage guidelines.
  • Weight range and lifespan: breed standard ranges and published breed lifespans.

Dewclaw is not a veterinarian. These figures are computed from published general-population formulas and breed tendencies.
