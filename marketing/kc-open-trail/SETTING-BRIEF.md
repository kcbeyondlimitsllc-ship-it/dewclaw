# Setting Brief: St. Helens, Oregon and Portland

Prepared 2026-11-16. All sources were accessed on 2026-11-16 unless a line says otherwise.

**This is research, not legal advice.** Nothing here has been reviewed by a lawyer. The naming question in section 1 needs a trademark or entertainment lawyer before any commercial launch.

## What the owner asked for, and where this brief lands

The request was: call the town "Halloweentown", use the movie, research the movie, and build the game around St. Helens, Oregon and Portland.

What the research supports:

- **Building the game around the real St. Helens and Portland: yes.** Real geography, public buildings, parks, bridges and history are free to use, and St. Helens really is a town that goes all-out for Halloween every October. That is a strong hook on its own.
- **Researching the movie: done** (sections 1 and 2).
- **Calling the town "Halloweentown" and using the movie's content: these are the two riskiest choices in the whole plan.** Everything the film added to the real town belongs to Disney, and Disney has a fifth film in early development as of October 2026.

This brief recommends a safer route that still delivers a Halloween town set in the film's real location. The decision on the name is the owner's. It is listed first in section 6.

Words used for evidence quality:

- **Confirmed**: read in the source and checked a second time.
- **Likely**: seen in one source, or only in a search summary of a blocked page.
- **Unconfirmed**: could not be verified. Do not rely on it.

## 1. Verdict on the four uses

### Summary

| # | Use | Risk | Recommended decision |
|---|-----|------|----------------------|
| A | Name the game town "Halloweentown" | Medium-high as an in-world place name only. High if it appears in the game title, URL, store page, logo, ads or anything sold. | Do not use it without a lawyer's sign-off or a Disney licence. Use the real name, St. Helens. |
| B | Film characters, plot, music, logo, stills | Very high | Do not use. Needs a Disney licence. |
| C | A plain factual line that the 1998 film was shot in St. Helens | Low, if done as described below | Use it, once, in plain text, with a non-affiliation line. |
| D | A generic autumn Halloween festival in a town called St. Helens | Low | Build the game on this. |

These risk levels are assessments drawn from the sources, not sourced facts and not legal advice.

### A. Naming the town "Halloweentown"

**Risk: medium-high in-world, high in any marketing or product use.**

Evidence:

- "Halloweentown" is the title of a 1998 Disney Channel film and the name of the fictional town inside it. It is a four-film series (1998, 2001, 2004, 2006) plus 2020 animated shorts. Confirmed. [Wikipedia, film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloweentown_(film)), [Wikipedia, series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloweentown_(film_series))
- The franchise is active. TheWrap reported on 2026-10-22 that a fifth film is in early development for Disney Channel and Disney+, with writers Lily Houghton and Ryan Koehn. It is not formally announced or greenlit. Confirmed as a report. [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/halloweentown-5-in-development-writers-disney/), [KATU](https://katu.com/news/entertainment/halloweentown-5-reportedly-in-development-at-disney)
- Wikipedia lists the owner as Disney Channel (The Walt Disney Company). The exact Disney entity that holds the copyright is unconfirmed; no US Copyright Office record was pulled.
- Disney's antipiracy policy says the company owns rights to "the characters, brands, titles and properties popularly associated with" Disney. Likely (seen in a search excerpt only). [Disney](https://impact.disney.com/resources/anti-piracy-policy/)
- **Trademark register: unconfirmed.** One search of USPTO data returned no record for the one-word mark HALLOWEENTOWN, but the search could not be reproduced. Treat "Disney has no registration" as unverified. Even if true, it is weak comfort: a series title can be registered and can be protected without registration. [USPTO on title refusals](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/laws/title-single-work-refusal-and-how-overcome-refusal). Applying that rule to this title is an inference, not something the source says.
- A different party holds a live registration for the two-word HALLOWEEN TOWN: Halloween Town, Inc. of Burbank, California, Reg. No. 4100506, class 35 (online retail of Halloween and horror merchandise), renewed 2021. Confirmed. So the name carries risk from more than one owner. [USPTO TSDR](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn77386937)
- The US Supreme Court ruled in 2023 (Jack Daniel's v. VIP Products) that the usual free-expression defence for creative works does not apply when a name is used as a brand for your own product. Confirmed. A town name that is also the game's selling point looks like that. [DWT summary](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2023/06/scotus-jack-daniels-trademark-rogers-test)
- The real city does not use the bare film title. Its festival is called "Spirit of Halloweentown". Confirmed. [Festival site](https://www.spiritofhalloweentown.co/event-info/about-spirit-of-halloweentown/)
- Disney's own D23 page for a 2023 screening in St. Helens says: "The Spirit of Halloweentown event and the attractions represented are not affiliated with The Walt Disney Company." Confirmed (dated 2023). [D23](https://tickets.d23.com/event/halloweentownsghjsaegr348le5jeelixc0hbdHUuSBp)
- The festival's 2024 accounts list a $5,000 "Film Licensing Fees" expense. Confirmed. The document does not say who was paid or for what, so it is unconfirmed that this is a Disney licence. Either way, whatever arrangement the city has would not cover a third-party game. [City memo, May 2025](https://mccmeetingspublic.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/sthelensor-meet-d784e7b8935b464dbaa2580e42665ad9/ITEM-Attachment-001-286ee997544b4ae0a94f77a0938222ac.pdf)
- Disney has shut down fan games, including free ones: Club Penguin Rewritten (2022), Club Penguin Online (2020), a Star Wars: KOTOR fan remake (2018), a Lorcana fan simulator (2024). Making money was cited as an aggravating factor. Likely. No case was found of Disney enforcing over this specific title. [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/2022/04/15/disney-gets-fans-who-made-their-own-club-penguin-online-game-arrested-over-copyright/)

**Recommended decision:** do not name the town "Halloweentown". Call it **St. Helens**, its real name, and let the festival carry the Halloween identity. If the owner still wants the film's name, the only two safe routes are a written Disney licence or a lawyer's written opinion. Without one of those, the name should not appear in the game title, domain, store listing, logo, trailer, ads, cosmetics or merchandise.

### B. Film characters, plot, music, logo, stills

**Risk: very high.**

Evidence: these are copyrighted expression owned by Disney. No fair-use argument covers reusing them wholesale in a commercial product, and Disney acts against fan games (see above). The legitimate route is a licence; Disney's studio licensing portal handles clips and stills and is unlikely to be practical for a small game. Likely. [Disney Studios Licensing](https://www.disneystudiolicensing.com/)

**Recommended decision:** use none of it. The full list is in section 2.

### C. Factual filming-location line

**Risk: low, if kept plain.**

Evidence:

- The 1998 film was shot in St. Helens and Scappoose over 24 days from mid-July 1998 (one Portland Monthly piece dates filming to 1997; sources differ). Likely. [IMDb locations](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0173886/locations/)
- Only the first film was shot in St. Helens. The sequels were shot in British Columbia and Utah. Confirmed via Wikipedia; no production records found. [Halloweentown High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloweentown_High)
- Ninth Circuit "nominative fair use" allows naming someone else's product to refer to it when you use only as much as needed and do nothing that suggests sponsorship. Likely (taken from a secondary summary, not the court opinion). [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2016/11/2016_11_16_second_circuit_expands_split__13324)

**Recommended decision:** use one line, in ordinary type, in the store description and credits:

> Set in the real St. Helens, Oregon, where the 1998 film Halloweentown was shot. This game is not affiliated with or endorsed by The Walt Disney Company.

Rules: no Disney logo, typeface, stills, music or character names. Not in the game's title, icon, key art or trailer headline. Do not market it as "the Halloweentown game". Do not claim any Portland landmark appears in the film; the Portland and Beaverton credits are unspecified.

### D. Generic autumn Halloween festival in St. Helens

**Risk: low.**

Evidence:

- Halloween, pumpkins, scarecrows, costumes, hay mazes and a town that celebrates all October are not owned by anyone.
- US copyright law lets anyone make pictures of buildings visible from public places (17 U.S.C. 120(a)). Confirmed. It covers buildings only, not sculptures, murals or signs. Whether an interactive 3D model counts as a "pictorial representation" is unconfirmed; ask the lawyer. [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/120)
- Using "St. Helens" and "Portland" as place names looks low risk, but that rests on general knowledge; no source was found on it. Unconfirmed.
- The real festival welcomes leashed, well-behaved dogs, and its help centre says pet costumes are "Highly encouraged". Confirmed. A dedicated pet costume contest at the festival is unconfirmed. [Festival help centre](https://spiritofhalloweentown.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/28793142573595-Can-my-pet-wear-a-costume)

Remaining risks to avoid: the city's festival name "Spirit of Halloweentown" and its branded attraction names; the film's exact plaza staging (section 2); and two Portland items (the Portlandia statue and the White Stag sign, section 3).

**Recommended decision:** build the game on this, combined with the factual line in C.

### Recommended names

- **Game town: St. Helens.** It is the real name, it is free, and it makes the factual film line true.
- **Festival: an original name.** Five options, none containing "Halloweentown":
  1. **Fog Bell Festival** (after the real 1855 fog bell displayed outside the courthouse)
  2. **Lantern Tide** (a river town lighting lanterns at dusk)
  3. **Old Plymouth Lantern Fair** (the town was founded as "Plymouth")
  4. **Pumpkin Moon Fair**
  5. **Harvest Howl** (dog-themed)

  None of these has been trademark-searched. Avoid "13 Nights": Disney holds 13 NIGHTS OF HALLOWEEN (Reg. 3317341) and the city runs a summer concert series called "13 Nights on the River".

- **What the player still gets:** a real riverside town that turns into a Halloween town every autumn, in the actual place the film was shot, with a truthful line saying so.

## 2. Do not copy

None of the following may appear in the game, its marketing or its cosmetics, by name or as a recognisable look-alike.

**Names and titles**

- "Halloweentown" as a town, game, festival, item or logo name.
- "Spirit of Halloweentown" (the city's festival brand) and its attraction names such as "Pumpkin Plaza", "Jack the Pumpkin" and "Boo Bash Pavilion".
- First-film characters: Aggie Cromwell, Gwen Piper, Marnie Piper, Dylan Piper, Sophie Piper, Luke, Kalabar, Benny, Harriet. Also the surnames Cromwell and Piper for any witch family.
- Sequel characters: Kal, Astrid, Alex, Gort, Ethan and Edgar Dalloway, Cody, Cassie, Principal Phil Flanagan, Pete, Chester, Natalie, Bobby, Scarlett, Sage, Sapphire, Silas Sinister, Chancellor Goodwin, Professor Periwinkle, Dr. Grogg, Aneesa, Burp-Urp-Snurt-Pfsfsfsfst III.
- "Halloweentown High", "Halloweentown University" and "Witch U". No monster high school or witch university.
- "Mortal World" and "Merlin's talisman".

**Designs and props**

- A skeleton who drives a taxi, or any wisecracking skeleton cabbie.
- A magical bus that travels between worlds, and a bus-stop signpost for it.
- A giant carved jack-o'-lantern in the courthouse plaza that lights up when an object is dropped inside it. Section 3 describes an original centrepiece to use instead.
- An illustrated witch storybook that reveals the town.
- A talisman or amulet that powers the town.
- A goblin turned into a handsome teenage boy.
- The film's creature townsfolk as a set: dentist-creature, pumpkin-headed residents, beings with extra heads or limbs.
- The film's title logo, typeface, poster art, stills and score.

**Scenes and story**

- A child who learns from a visiting grandmother that she is a witch.
- Siblings sneaking onto a bus to follow her.
- Townspeople vanishing and being found frozen inside an abandoned cinema.
- A "closed forever" theatre.
- Gathering werewolf hair, ghost sweat and a vampire fang for a potion.
- A hooded villain revealed as the mayor.
- The backstory of a town founded a thousand years ago by monsters escaping humans, reachable only on Halloween until a portal seals at midnight.

**Also on the same streets: Twilight (2008, a different studio)**

- The fictional business names "Petite Jolie", "Thunderbird and Whale" and "Bloated Toad", and any "Bella's house" label. The real buildings may be drawn; those names may not. Street addresses given in fan guides are unconfirmed.

**Real private places**

- Do not label any house as a film character's home. A generic Victorian house on a hill is fine.

Other productions said to have filmed in St. Helens (Behind the Mask, Everyman's War, To My Daughter with Love) are unconfirmed. Do not cite them.

## 3. Game map plan

### Overall layout

A stylised, compressed map, north up, drawn on a 10 x 10 grid. Columns A to J run west to east. Rows 1 to 10 run north to south.

`~~~` is water. The Columbia River forms the east edge of St. Helens and flows north. The Multnomah Channel (`chan`) runs up from Portland behind Sauvie Island and joins the Columbia at St. Helens.

Rough proportions of playable area:

| Region | Grid | Share |
|--------|------|-------|
| St. Helens town, riverfront and parks | A1 to F4 | about 35% |
| Highway 30 corridor and Scappoose stop | A5 to D8 | about 15% |

| Portland district | C8 to I10 | about 30% |

In the real town, Highway 30 runs along the inland west side, about 1.5 miles from the water. The old town sits at the far east edge on the river. The real core is roughly 2 miles north to south (an estimate worked out from published coordinates). Keep that shape: highway strip on the west, street grid in the middle, historic waterfront on the east.

Skybox: Mount St. Helens to the north-east across the river, Mount Adams further behind it (visibility stated by the city for the Riverfront District only), Mount Hood to the east-south-east. Mount Hood is 11,249 ft and about 50 miles from Portland.

Main source for the St. Helens layout: the city's [Parks and Trails brochure and map](https://www.sthelensoregon.gov/DocumentCenter/View/906/Parks-and-Trails-Brochure). Confirmed.

### Weather

Portland gets about 37 inches of rain a year at the airport. November to March bring 5 to 6 inches a month; July and August about half an inch each. Likely. Default to dry clear summers with the mountains visible, and drizzle and wet streets from October onward. That suits the festival season. [NWS normals](https://www.weather.gov/media/pqr/climate/ClimateBookPortland/pg94.pdf)

## 4. Missions and festival activities

All wholesome and dog-themed. No crime, no weapons, no villains from the film.

### 16 missions

1. **Ring the Old Bell** (courthouse plaza). Tutorial. Learn to run, jump and bark by reaching the fog bell before the clock tower finishes chiming.
2. **Plaza Paper Round** (S 1st Street). Deliver the morning paper to ten shopfronts before they open.
3. **Riverwalk Patrol** (Columbia View Park to Nob Hill). Walk the riverfront trail, greet five visitors and pick up dropped litter.
4. **Island Shuttle** (North Marina to Sand Island). Ride the shuttle boat and carry a forgotten tent bag to a camper.
5. **Sandcastle Judge** (Sand Island beach). Help build the tallest sandcastle by digging and fetching buckets. Based on the real August sandcastle competition.
6. **Lighthouse Lunch** (Warrior Rock Light). Carry a lunch pail along the 3-mile island trail to the keeper without dropping it.
7. **Oak Woods Sniff Trail** (Nob Hill Nature Park). Follow a scent trail on-leash to find a walker's lost glove.
8. **Creek Chase** (McCormick Park). Follow Milton Creek downstream and retrieve five runaway disc-golf discs.
9. **Quarry Echo** (the Old Quarry). Climb the basalt ledges and bark at three echo points to wake a sleepy foreman for his shift.
10. **Mill Whistle** (the Paper Mill). Carry paper rolls between loading bays before the whistle blows.
11. **Beat the Train** (Highway 30). Race the freight train on foot along the safe side path from Scappoose to the next crossing.
12. **Pumpkin Haul** (Wapato Patch Farm). Pull a small cart of pumpkins from the field to the farm stand.
13. **Lost in the Corn** (Wapato Patch Farm). Guide three lost children out of the corn maze by barking so they can follow.
14. **Bridge Flags** (St. Johns Bridge and Cathedral Park). Collect ten ribbon flags along the bridge walkway and bring them to the park stage.
15. **Three-Dog Walk** (Forest Park). Take a dog-walking job: lead three dogs along the Wildwood Trail and keep their leads untangled.
16. **Cart Runner** (Portland food cart pod). Deliver hot orders from four carts to picnic tables before they go cold, then earn the first payment toward your own treat cart.

### 8 festival-season activities

Active from late September to 31 October in game time, on weekends. The real festival ran six weekends in 2026 (Sept 26 to Nov 1). [Festival site](https://spiritofhalloweentown.co/)

1. **Lantern Stack lighting.** Every evening, pull the rope with other dogs to raise the lights on the plaza centrepiece.
2. **Costume parade.** Walk the stage in Columbia View Park in the player's own outfit; the crowd votes. This is the game's invention. A real pet costume contest at the festival is unconfirmed.
3. **Hay maze.** A timed maze on the plaza with hidden treats.
4. **Pumpkin bowling.** Roll small pumpkins at stacked gourds by nosing them down a lane.
5. **Scarecrow and window contest.** Carry decorations to shopfronts on S 1st Street and help each shop finish its display.
6. **Market stall helper.** Run change and parcels between about 70 craft stalls.
7. **Lantern river cruise.** Ride a decorated boat from Courthouse Dock around Sand Island and spot ten floating lanterns.
8. **Trolley tour guide.** Ride the town trolley and bark at each real landmark (courthouse, fog bell, quarry, lighthouse view) to trigger the guide's history line. No film-location stops.

The real festival also has a pirate encampment with cannon demonstrations. Leave the cannons out to keep the no-weapons rule.
